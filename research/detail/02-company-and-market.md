More detail from 2025 [V]:
- EBITDA was DKK 3.80m, down from 6.31m in 2024.
- Finance expenses were DKK 2.04m, up from 0.04m. The accounting policies say these include exchange losses. A weaker USD in 2025 is a likely driver [E].
- **Deferred income (prepaid subscriptions) was DKK 31.6m, up from 22.8m (+39%).** This is a strong sign of growth in annual-in-advance SaaS billings and bookings [E interpretation].
- Trade receivables were DKK 14.4m, up from 12.3m.
- Wages and salaries were DKK 19.7m. "Other staff cost" was DKK 17.0m.
- Management called the result "satisfactory".

**Interpretation [E]:**
- **Growth.** Gross profit grew about 11x from 2015 to 2019, when the company was on perpetual licences. It dipped in 2020–2021, which coincides with COVID and the October 2020 switch from perpetual licences to subscriptions with the Attain launch; a subscription switch normally depresses recognised revenue for a while. Gross profit then recovered: +32.5% (2022), +16.9% (2023), +13.8% (2024), and **+5.1% (2025)**. Gross-profit growth slowed in 2025, but deferred income jumped 39%, which points to stronger forward bookings.
- **Profitability.** Margins are thin. Net profit has been between -3.2m and +4.2m DKK since 2021.
- **Size.** Gross profit of DKK 41.3m is roughly €5.5m or US$6.0–6.3m. Revenue is unknown but higher. Latka claims "$11.3M revenue, 75 employees" [3P, unverified]. A plausible range for group revenue is US$7–11m [E].
- **Employee count.** The Danish entity averaged 26 FTE in 2025 [V]. Proff and estatistik show a current figure of 29–31 [3P/registry]. Including the US subsidiary and the India and Australia teams, the group probably has 50–80 people [E]. Aggregators: Prospeo 51–100 (counts 59), Clodura 51–200, Latka 75, Kompass 20–49 (DK only) [3P] ([prospeo](https://prospeo.io/c/clicklearn); Latka via search).

**Product summary [V]:**
- **Record-once, publish-many authoring** in a Windows desktop app ("Attain Studio"). It produces **7 learning formats**: ShowMe, TryMe and TestMe videos; online and offline docs (Word, PDF, HTML); PowerPoint; and a virtual assistant.
- **Replay** re-captures screenshots after system updates to refresh documentation automatically. This is the company's core differentiator.
- Auto-translation into 45 languages. Older materials claim 118 languages and 600+ narrators.
- Learning portals, either cloud-hosted (ContentCloud) or on-prem.
- **UXP/UAC** in-app guidance plus an AI chatbot.
- APT test automation.
- AI avatars through Colossyan.
- Analytics dashboard.
- Chrome/Edge extension (v3).
- Connectors: D365 F&O/AX, BC/NAV and CE/CRM; Power Platform; M365; IFS; SAP; Oracle; Salesforce; Acumatica; custom Windows and web apps. ClickLearn claims coverage of about 90% of Windows and web apps.

Sources: [home](https://www.clicklearn.com/), [features](https://www.clicklearn.com/product/features/), [integrations](https://www.clicklearn.com/integrations/).

- **Common cons:**
  - Steep authoring learning curve.

  - Recording or replay is brittle when fields change. Trackpad clicks are sometimes missed.
  - Large projects publish slowly.
  - The IFS replay for other languages is "clunky".

  - The UI feels dated.
  - Deploying in-app assistants can need vendor help.
  - Few reviews, many of them old.

**Differentiators [E, synthesis]:**
1. Captures processes across ERP desktop and web clients, notably Dynamics F&O/BC, IFS, Oracle and SAP.
2. One recording produces 7 formats in 45 languages.
3. **Replay** auto-updates content for frequent release waves (Dynamics, IFS twice-yearly releases, Acumatica R1/R2). This underpins its "update-cycle" messaging.
4. Process recordings are reused for **automated regression testing (APT)**, which is rare among DAPs.
5. A channel model aligned with ERP partners (Microsoft, IFS).
6. A price point well below WalkMe and Whatfix.
7. Strong support ratings.

**Weaknesses and risks [E, synthesis]:**
1. Small scale: about DKK 41m gross profit, roughly 26 FTE in Denmark plus offshore staff. Thin profitability limits R&D compared with Whatfix, WalkMe and Scribe.
2. Absent from analyst reports, with few and ageing reviews.
3. Windows-only authoring client and a dated UX.
4. AI features are integration-led (Colossyan; chatbot grounded in customer content) and lag DAP leaders that promote AI agents.
5. Analytics are weaker than overlay DAPs.
6. Exposed to Microsoft Copilot and in-product guidance commoditising "how-to" content.
7. Dependent on the ERP-partner channel.
8. The website carries outdated claims (Gold Partner badge, counters showing 0, a stale careers page).
9. Founder-owned with dividends paid out and no outside capital, so it has limited firepower for M&A or a growth push. It is also a plausible acquisition target for an ERP-ecosystem consolidator or PE roll-up [E].
